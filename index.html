Experimental Study

                    

                

            


            
            

                

                     Research Questions
                

                

                    

                        
RQ1: Can rule-based heuristics detect prompt intent with reasonable accuracy?

                    

                    

                        
RQ2: What is the relationship between token overhead and prompt quality?

                    

                    

                        
RQ3: Is there a principled "quality-to-cost ratio" for prompt optimization?

                    

                    

                        
RQ4: When does optimization help vs. hurt? Can we predict failure cases?

                    

                

            


            
            

                

                     Limitations & Honest Assessment
                

                

                    
This is an exploratory project, not a solved problem:

                    
    
                        
  •  No LLM-in-the-loop validation of output quality
    • 
                        
  •  Quality scores are heuristic proxies, not ground truth
    • 
                        
  •  Intent detection is keyword-based and brittle (~75% accuracy)
    • 
                        
  •  Token estimation may be off by ±15-25%
    • 
                    

                    
I built this to understand the problem space, not because I have the answer.

                

            


            
            

                

                     Key Observations
                

                

                    

                        
Observation 1: Minimal changes often suffice. Adding just a role tag feels surprisingly effective for many tasks.

                    

                    

                        
Observation 2: Token costs add up fast. Chain-of-thought scaffolding alone adds 80-150 tokens.

                    

                    

                        
Observation 3: Intent detection is the weak link. Most failures trace back to misclassified intent.

                    

                    

                        
Open question: Does structural improvement actually help? I haven't validated this with real LLM outputs.

                    

                

            


            
            

                

                     Surprising Finding: When Optimization Hurts
                

                

                    
In ~15% of my qualitative tests (n≈25), optimized prompts performed worse than originals:

                    
    
                        
  •  Simple queries: Adding scaffolding to "What is the capital of France?" produced unnecessary verbosity
    • 
                        
  •  Over-constrained creative tasks: Maximum mode made creative outputs feel formulaic
    • 
                        
  •  Misclassified intent: Wrong techniques applied when detector failed
    • 
                    

                    
Implication: Knowing when NOT to optimize may be harder than optimization itself.

Research-Driven Development

                
This project synthesizes insights from 15+ peer-reviewed papers on prompt engineering, combining Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al.), Tree of Thoughts (Yao et al.), and novel cost-optimization techniques into a unified, production-ready system.
